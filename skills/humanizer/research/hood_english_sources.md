# Hood English sources

Research run 2026-09-16. The rules in `../hood_english.md` cite these.

## African American English grammar
1. Rickford, "The Creole Origins of African American Vernacular English: Evidence from copula absence" — web.stanford.edu/~rickford/papers/CreoleOriginsOfAAVE.html. Constraint hierarchy NP < locative < adjective < V-ing < gonna; first person `am` absent under 1% in urban AAVE; past tense copula rarely or never absent.
2. Sharma & Rickford, "AAVE/Creole copula absence" (2009) — johnrickford.com/Portals/45/documents/papers/Sharma-and-Rickford-2009a-AAVE-Creole-Copula-Absence.pdf. `I'm` categorically present, `*I the man` ungrammatical.
3. Rickford, "AAVE in California: Over four decades of variationist research" (2015) — johnrickford.com/portals/45/documents/papers/Rickford%202015b%20AAVE%20in%20California-Over%20Four%20Decades%20of%20Vibrant%20Variationist%20Research.pdf. Variable, rule-governed features.
4. Labov, "Contraction, deletion, and inherent variability of the English copula" (Language 45, 1969); summary via Labov, "Unendangered Dialects, Endangered People" — ling.upenn.edu/~wlabov/Papers/UDEP.pdf. Deletion only where standard English contracts; exposed position keeps `is`.
5. Green, *African American English: A Linguistic Introduction* (Cambridge, 2002) — cambridge.org/core/books/african-american-english/1AE59657F9CF1BBC3A2BF2B9BB29D1D0. Habitual `be`, stressed `BIN` as remote past, `done`, `finna`, negative concord, existential `it`, tense-aspect as a system.
6. Green, "African American English", in *Language in the USA* (Cambridge, 2004) — cambridge.org/core/books/language-in-the-usa/african-american-english/12F2798CD781956AD9D5CEF9841B301A. BIN distinct from present-perfect `been`.
7. Language Log, "Presentational/static locatives or 'go' copulas in AAVE" — languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/nll/?p=8358. Copula variation discussion.

## Multicultural London English
8. Cheshire, Kerswill, Fox & Torgersen, "Contact, the feature pool and the speech community: The emergence of Multicultural London English" (J. Sociolinguistics 15, 2011); project overview in Cheshire, "Multicultural London English", Wiley Encyclopedia of World Englishes — onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9781119518297.eowe00013.
9. Cheshire, "Grammaticalisation in social context: the emergence of a new English pronoun" (J. Sociolinguistics 17, 2013); summarised in "Recent changes in London English", Complutense Journal of English Studies — revistas.ucm.es/index.php/CJES/article/view/77504. `man` as first-person pronoun, recorded from 2001.
10. Pichler, "Grammaticalization and language contact in a discourse-pragmatic change in progress: the spread of innit in London English", Language in Society — cambridge.org/core/journals/language-in-society/article/grammaticalization-and-language-contact-in-a-discoursepragmatic-change-in-progress-the-spread-of-innit-in-london-english/BF2579426AFD6EC33308D6F52113F2D9. Invariant `innit` attached to any anchor.
11. Cheshire, "Taking the longer view: Explaining MLE and Multicultural Paris French" (2020) — onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/josl.12385. `this is me` quotative, feature pool.
12. MEITS policy paper, "Multicultural London English and social and educational policies" — meits.org/policy-papers/paper/multicultural-london-english-and-social-and-educational-policies.
13. londonxlondon, "Peng tings: an A-Z of roadman slang" — londonxlondon.com/roadman-slang/. Glosses for wagwan (Patois), peng, bare, peak, allow it, wasteman, mandem. Corroboration only.

## Texting orthography and emoji
25. Gunraj, Drumm-Hewitt, Dashow, Upadhyay & Klin, "Texting insincerely: The role of the period in text messaging", Computers in Human Behavior 55 (2016) — sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563215302181. One-word replies with a period rated less sincere; no effect in handwriting.
26. McCulloch, *Because Internet* (Riverhead, 2019) — en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Because_Internet ; chapter summary via electricliterature.com/why-ok-is-the-most-terrifying-text-you-could-ever-receive/. Typographical tone of voice: lowercase as deadpan, caps as shouting, letter repetition, `ok.` as cold.
27. Dictionary.com, "How Gen Z uses emoji" — dictionary.com/e/gen-z-explains-emoji-to-millennials/. `💀` as "I'm dead", `😭` as overwhelmed positive.
28. Wikipedia, "Skull emoji" — en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skull_emoji. McCulloch on laughter markers wearing out; `😂` read as older.
