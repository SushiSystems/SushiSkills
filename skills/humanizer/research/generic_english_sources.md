# English sources

## Style authorities

2. Orwell, "Politics and the English Language" — orwellfoundation.com
3. Williams & Bizup, *Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace* (characters as subjects, actions as verbs, old-before-new, stress position)

8. Google developer documentation style guide, voice and tone — developers.google.com/style/tone
9. digital.gov plain language guides — digital.gov/guides/plain-language

## Corpus and detection research
10. Kobak, González-Márquez, Horvát & Lause, "Delving into ChatGPT usage in academic writing through excess vocabulary" — arxiv.org/abs/2406.07016. 14M PubMed abstracts; 2024 excess vocabulary is style words (66% verbs, 18% adjectives). delves r=25.2, showcasing r=9.2, underscores r=9.1; potential δ=0.041, findings δ=0.027, crucial δ=0.026.
11. Liang et al., "Monitoring AI-Modified Content at Scale" (ICML 2024) — proceedings.mlr.press/v235/liang24b.html. 6.5–16.9% of reviews at ICLR/NeurIPS/CoRL/EMNLP LLM-modified; correlated with lower reviewer confidence.
12. Juzek & Ward, "Why Does ChatGPT 'Delve' So Much?" (COLING 2025) — aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.426/. RLHF is the likely driver; raters prefer the overused words.
13. Juzek & Ward, "Word Overuse and Alignment in LLMs" — arxiv.org/abs/2508.01930
14. Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing (WikiProject AI Cleanup) — en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing. Era-versioned word lists; structural and formatting tells; ~10% drop in is/are.
15. "Beyond Checkmate: exploring creative chokepoints in AI text" — arxiv.org/pdf/2501.19301 (burstiness, perplexity)
16. "Stylometry can reveal artificial intelligence authorship" — ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12558491/
