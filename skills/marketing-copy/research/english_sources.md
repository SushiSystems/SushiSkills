# English sources

Literature review run 2026-09-17. `../SKILL.md` and `../english.md` cite these. Each entry was
found on the publisher, DOI or project page by a research agent; full texts were not read line
by line. Entries marked UNVERIFIED were not confirmed.

## Trust in AI-sounding copy
1. Jakesch, Hancock & Naaman (2023), "Human heuristics for AI-generated language are flawed", PNAS — pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10089155. People cannot detect AI text; they read first person, contractions and personal topics as human.
2. Schilke & Reimann (2025), "The transparency dilemma: How AI disclosure erodes trust" — experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/the-transparency-dilemma-how-ai-disclosure-erodes-trust/. Thirteen preregistered experiments; disclosed AI use lowers trust.
3. Journal of Business Research 186 (2025), seven experiments on marketing messages believed to be AI-written; lower word of mouth and loyalty — ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbrese/v186y2025ics0148296324004880.html. Title and authors not recorded.
4. Klaviyo and Datalily consumer survey (December 2025), reported by eMarketer; noticed AI content lowers trust in 31% and raises it in 7% — emarketer.com/content/visible-ai-marketing-four-times-more-likely-cost-brands-trust-than-build. Industry survey, not peer reviewed.
5. Kobak et al., excess vocabulary in PubMed abstracts — arxiv.org/abs/2406.07016. Verified in `humanizer/research/generic_english_sources.md`.
6. Wikipedia, "Signs of AI writing" (WikiProject AI Cleanup) — en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing.

## Credibility and order
7. Haugtvedt & Wegener (1994), "Message Order Effects in Persuasion", Journal of Consumer Research 21(1) — doi.org/10.1086/209393. Primacy dominates when readers elaborate.
8. Friestad & Wright (1994), "The Persuasion Knowledge Model", Journal of Consumer Research 21(1) — doi.org/10.1086/209380.
9. Aaker, Vohs & Mogilner (2010), "Nonprofits Are Seen as Warm and For-Profits as Competent", Journal of Consumer Research 37(2) — doi.org/10.1086/651566. Mission framing lowers perceived competence; one competence cue closes the gap.
10. Hansen & Wänke (2010), "Truth From Language and Truth From Fit", Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin 36(11) — doi.org/10.1177/0146167210386238. Concrete wording is judged more probably true.
11. Eisend (2006), "Two-Sided Advertising: A Meta-Analysis", International Journal of Research in Marketing 23(2) — doi.org/10.1016/j.ijresmar.2005.11.001.
12. Chen, Yao & Kotha (2009), "Entrepreneur Passion and Preparedness in Business Plan Presentations", Academy of Management Journal 52(1) — doi.org/10.5465/amj.2009.36462018. Preparedness, not passion, predicts funding.
13. Carton, Murphy & Clark (2014), "A (Blurry) Vision of the Future", Academy of Management Journal 57(6). Image-based vision language outperforms abstract language.
14. Camerer, Loewenstein & Weber (1989), "The Curse of Knowledge in Economic Settings", Journal of Political Economy 97(5). UNVERIFIED venue: the research agent cited American Economic Review; check before quoting.
